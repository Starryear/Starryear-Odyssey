Starryear-Odyssey-Photo-Diptych
星年奥德赛双联画
Author: Starryear
Version: V5 — Clean Epic Edition (26 AUG 2026)

How to use: Upload a photograph that you have the legal right to use, then copy the [FULL PROMPT] below. If the platform cannot complete “generate the lower panel + composite the original photograph + typeset the labels correctly” in one pass, the process must be split into two steps: first generate a completely text-free abstract lower panel, then use a reliable layout or compositing tool to assemble the final diptych.

[FULL PROMPT | COPY FROM HERE]

Using the photograph I uploaded in this turn as the sole evidence of reality, create a vertical diptych combining “original photography × an episode from Homer’s Odyssey × contemporary abstract art.” This is not a generic filter, film poster, illustrated photograph, mythological character illustration, or oil-painted version of the source. First understand the photograph, then identify the Odyssey episode that fits it most closely, and finally reconstruct the source photograph’s counts, distances, directions, light, color, and spatial pressure as an original abstract painting.

This edition follows a “Clean Epic” direction. Epic force must come from scale, enclosure, paths, repetition, material weight, and near–far relationships—not from smoke, grime, grain, scratches, black-and-gold grading, or unmotivated debris. The image should be sparse but exact, structurally clear, and traceable to key evidence, with broad material areas carried primarily by readable midtones.

1. FINAL STRUCTURE

Output only one complete upper-and-lower diptych placed on a unified deep-black archival canvas:

1. Upper half: the user-uploaded original photograph.
2. Lower half: an original full-bleed abstract painting generated from the source evidence and the selected Odyssey episode.

The two panels must connect directly, with no white divider or obvious border. The complete diptych must sit within a unified near-black archival canvas: retain a restrained outer margin approximately 2%–4% of the source photograph’s width on all sides, plus a black title band at the bottom approximately 8%–10% of the source photograph’s width. Use a warm near-black or charcoal close to #0F0F0E or #121211; do not use a blue-purple commercial-poster gradient. The black belongs only to the outer layout. It must not cover, darken, or alter the upper photograph, and it must not become an excuse for large areas of dead black in the lower panel.

At the seam, the topmost part of the lower panel may use colors, luminance, direction, and texture already present along the source photograph’s bottom edge to form a short natural transition. That transition must occur entirely inside the lower panel and must never cover, soften, or alter any pixel of the upper photograph.

2. THE UPPER PANEL MUST PRESERVE THE ORIGINAL PHOTOGRAPH

The upper panel must use the uploaded original photograph directly. Do not replace it with an AI-generated look-alike, and do not:

- crop, stretch, distort, or change its aspect ratio;
- redraw or replace subjects, or modify people or animals;
- change exposure, contrast, hue, saturation, or white balance;
- add cinematic grading, grain, glow, smoke, candlelight, or any other effect;
- add a title, number, watermark, border, or ornament.

These prohibitions apply to text, borders, and decoration placed inside the photograph. The unified near-black archival canvas outside the photograph and the bottom title band remain required parts of the final layout.

The original photograph is evidence of the real world and the narrative starting point of the work. If the platform cannot guarantee that it will remain unchanged, generate the lower panel separately and then composite it with the source using a deterministic image-layout tool.

3. REQUIRED PHOTOGRAPH ANALYSIS BEFORE GENERATION

Observe and record the following internally before generating. Do not place this analysis in the finished image:

1. Primary subjects: people, animals, architecture, water, roads, trees, flames, doorways, vessels, or other objects.
2. Counts and relationships: isolation, mutual gaze, enclosure, pursuit, gathering, waiting, repetition, passage, return, or pressure from both sides.
3. Dominant direction: advance, backward glance, ascent, descent, traverse, rotation, convergence, escape, or stasis.
4. Spatial structure: open sea, narrow passage, cave, palace, forest, island, threshold, abyss, fence, or occupied public space.
5. Light structure: where light enters, the brightest area, shadow coverage, reflection path, and whether the light is natural, candlelit, or overcast/liminal.
6. Color evidence: extract at least a dominant color, shadow color, midtone, highlight, and a real accent color present in the source.
7. Emotional pressure: temptation, danger, forgetting, vigilance, prophecy, sacrifice, homecoming, solitude, pressure before a storm, or calm in the face of fate.

4. SELECTING AN ODYSSEY EPISODE

The selection must be supported by at least two or three pieces of photographic evidence. Do not select a sea voyage merely because the image contains water. Do not force a monster into the work for drama, and do not stack every famous Odyssey symbol into one image.

Possible routes include:

- ATHENA KEEPS WATCH: a solitary or vigilant animal, distant figure, elevated viewpoint, observer, or single eye-like point.
- AFTER TROY: harbor, wall, masts, crowd, departing road, traces of war, or an immense timber structure.
- LOTUS SHORE: slow-moving water, quiet gathering, drifting nodes, dreamlike stasis, forgetting, and lingering.
- CYCLOPS CAVE: cave mouth, circular structure, giant single mass, one high-contrast eye ring, or small subjects surrounded by a larger form.
- BAG OF WINDS: clouds, airflow, spirals, windblown fabric, radiating lines, and directions falling out of control.
- CIRCE’S ISLAND: animals, garden, forest, ornate interior, seductive warm light, and altered identity.
- SIREN OMENS: a flock of birds, repeated sound-like lines, a distant call, or a goal that cannot safely be reached.
- BETWEEN TWO MONSTERS: a narrow passage, pressure from both sides, vortex, cave, or tendril-like paths.
- CATTLE OF THE SUN: grouped animals, severe sunlight, golden fields, or an object that must not be touched.
- DESCENT TO THE UNDERWORLD: flame, darkness, reflection on water, bare trees, underground space, smoke, or red-black tension.
- CALYPSO’S ISLAND: an isolated person within a beautiful but inescapable place and an extended period of suspension.
- HALL OF SUITORS: dense crowds, repeated seating, halls, banquets, or an occupied public space.
- PENELOPE’S VIGIL / PENELOPE’S LOOM: windows, fabric, spiderwebs, clocks, empty rooms, prolonged waiting, and repeated weaving and unweaving.
- THE HIDDEN KING: a doorway, back-facing figure, obscured person, unnoticed observer, or hidden return.
- THE BOW TRIAL: arcs, tension lines, aligned targets, and sudden directional release.
- THE LONG RETURN: coast, road, navigation marker, distant traveler, solitary vessel, or a clear route home.

If no specific episode is sufficiently accurate, use a broader but truthful proposition such as THE WAITING HOUSE, A DANGEROUS PASSAGE, BEFORE THE HOMECOMING, or THE SEA REMEMBERS.

5. ABSTRACT TRANSLATION OF THE LOWER PANEL

The lower panel must not redraw the photograph at a smaller scale, nor may it be merely an oil-paint, watercolor, printmaking, or filter effect. Translate objects into relationships:

- Water, road, railing, or ground → tidal color bands, navigational routes, arcs, or layered planes spanning the composition.
- People or animals → small upright marks, ovals, seed-like nodes, isolated color fields, gazes, or directional coordinates.
- Architecture and doorways → dark rectangles, repeated arches, vertical tower forms, layered planes, and occupied intervals.
- Tree trunks, masts, railings, or crowds → irregularly spaced vertical lines, slender rods, point fields, and shifts in density.
- Flame → broken ascending bands, dark-red fissures, bone-white heated edges, bronze nodes, and smoke-black paths.
- Vortex, giant mouth, or dangerous strait → incomplete rings, curved clamps, or consuming but textured dark apertures.
- Eye or observer → one high-contrast focal point, ring structure, or aperture surrounded by subordinate elements.
- Reflection → shortened, faded, displaced, or broken vertical echoes.
- Group → repeated modules with changing density; never arrange them in a mechanically regular grid.
- Light and shadow → diagonal cuts, elongated light bands, fractured grids, or tilted structures.

For animals, preserve only their real count, spacing, posture, gaze, or direction. Do not enlarge shells, fur, eyes, claws, or complete silhouettes into a decorative centerpiece. Unless both the photograph and the selected episode make it indispensable, a “central shell-pattern ornament” is a failed result.

6. ICONIC ODYSSEY MOTIFS

Each work may include one to three motifs strictly associated with the selected episode, but they must be fused with structures already present in the photograph rather than floating on top like stickers:

- Trojan Horse: a colossal, rough, ribbed timber mass or gate-like silhouette; appropriate to departure, aftermath of war, harbors, and immense architectural structures.
- Cyclops: one gigantic incomplete eye ring, cave aperture, or single high-contrast point.
- Charybdis: a consuming vortex, sinking circulation, and paths being dragged inward.
- Scylla: six attack vectors or toothed wedges of unequal height and spacing, pressing inward from the side; do not depict a complete monster.
- Sirens: repeated sound-like fine lines, birdlike fragments, or a distant lure point; do not depict fantasy siren women.
- Penelope’s loom: warp and weft, repeatedly woven and unwoven threads, a center of waiting, and an incomplete grid.
- Bow trial: a monumental arc, tensile force, aligned targets, and sudden directional release.
- Underworld: a descending path, dark river, incomplete doorway, smoke lit by fire, stone, and bone-white heated edges.

Do not add Greek temples, helmets, armor, spears, tridents, warships, or multiple conflicting famous symbols without evidence.

7. COLOR MUST CORRESPOND TO THE SOURCE

The lower panel’s palette is not a fixed template. Approximately 70%–85% of the image should come from the source photograph’s dominant colors, midtones, and shadow colors; approximately 10%–25% should come from its highlights; and the mythic accent should generally occupy no more than 5%–10%. If the source contains a vivid red, orange, green, gold, or blue, prioritize that color as a navigation mark, danger signal, eye point, fissure, or light source.

Do not force every photograph into blue-orange or black-and-gold grading to make it feel “epic.” Daylight scenes must preserve sky, water, stone, vegetation, and atmospheric distance. Interiors and night scenes may use candlelight, but must still retain the source photograph’s color identity.

8. CANDLELIGHT IS AN IMPORTANT NARRATIVE SOURCE, NOT AN ORANGE FILTER

When the scene permits, introduce an “Odyssey ember”: beeswax gold, brazier orange, burnt umber, dark red, or oxidized bronze emerging from a source-justified doorway, fissure, arch, rope knot, timber rib, node, gap in smoke, or reflection on water.

Candlelight must illuminate nearby material so that the viewer can read stone, wood, rope, smoke, fabric, metal, or water reflection. It must not appear as a few orange dots floating in a field of black. Use one principal light source and no more than three faint echoes, with believable falloff.

Choose one exposure mode according to the photograph:

1. Firelit mode: caves, palaces, the Underworld, the waiting house, and night thresholds. Preserve readable stone, wood, smoke, and water midtones; unless the source itself contains an equivalent black field, keep near-black coverage preferably below 25% and fully dead-black coverage preferably below 8%.
2. Natural-light mode: coasts, open water, islands, roads, gardens, and daylight travel. Preserve pale sky, steel-blue water, sunlit stone, natural greens, and atmospheric perspective. Embers may appear only as small narrative punctuation.
3. Overcast/liminal-light mode: mist, forest, glass reflections, waiting, and suspended time. Maintain broad readable midtones and one controlled opening of light.

If the source is already high-key and cool, and adding candlelight would clearly break its color logic, omit candlelight. Do not compensate by creating dead black.

9. COMPOSITIONAL DENSITY OF THE LOWER PANEL

The lower panel must form a complete visual world:

- at least one dominant large-scale structure;
- two to four groups of medium-scale rhythm;
- a small number of tiny nodes establishing direction and depth;
- preservation of the source photograph’s asymmetrical relationships;
- a clear visual entry, a zone of tension or danger, and an exit direction;
- every area participates in the narrative, while meaningful pauses and breathing room remain possible.

Do not center everything, distribute elements evenly, make the composition mechanically symmetrical, or place one undersized form in the middle while leaving the rest of the field inert.

Also do not fill the image with decorative smoke, ash, splatter, distressed scratches, fake film damage, dense gravel, or excessive grain. Real materials may have texture, but that texture must be restrained, clean, and serve the weight of stone, wood, metal, fabric, water, and paper.

10. JUDGMENT REFERENCES DERIVED FROM 16 ACTUAL WORKS

The following cases demonstrate how to infer narrative and form from photographic evidence. They are not fixed templates. Every new photograph must be analyzed afresh; do not mechanically copy these compositions.

1. Bridge, water, straight railing, and distant vehicles → THE LONG RETURN. Translate the railing into a homeward navigation line, the bridge beam into a threshold of fate, and the warm reflection on water into a return beacon; combine natural light with a restrained ember.
2. White walls, layered eaves, bare branches, and a doorway → PENELOPE’S VIGIL. Translate the stepped eaves into loom-like tiers, the branches into unfinished warp and weft, and place a small amount of beeswax gold inside the doorway; use overcast/liminal light.
3. Red-brick hall, repeated arches, steps, and surrounding trees → HALL OF SUITORS. Translate repeated doorways and steps into the rhythm of an occupied hall, using dense nodes to signify the suitors; let candlelight reveal stone rather than sink into black.
4. Night temple, lanterns, dense points of fire, and a deep-blue sky → DESCENT TO THE UNDERWORLD. Translate the fire points into a descending ritual, smoke columns, and the entrance to a dark river; preserve the deep blue and wood-brown while fire reveals smoke, timber, and stone.
5. Stream, rocks, two turtles, and small floating points → LOTUS SHORE. Translate the two turtles only into two seed-like coordinates, the water flow into slow color bands, and the floating points into nodes of forgetting; do not enlarge shell patterns.
6. Coast, rocks, red railing, and three people → BETWEEN TWO MONSTERS. Translate the red railing into a danger boundary, the opposing rocky shores into the pressure of Scylla and the vortex of Charybdis, and the people into passage nodes; natural sea light remains dominant.
7. Forking rail tracks, platform, train, and yellow signal → A DANGEROUS PASSAGE. Translate the rails into branching paths of fate, the yellow light into the sole navigation node, and the overhead lines into a tensile web; use overcast/liminal light.
8. Forest, three pavilions, and one isolated seated figure → CIRCE’S ISLAND. Translate the repeated pavilion roofs into seductive island nodes and the forest into a beautiful enclosed place of lingering; use natural green light with a restrained warm-gold accent.
9. Fountain at the center of a rock field, circular water column, and high-contrast focal point → CYCLOPS CAVE. Translate the fountain and stone ring into one colossal incomplete eye ring and cave light shaft; do not depict a complete giant.
10. Children by the sea, distant boat, long breakwater, and enormous horizontal structure → AFTER TROY. Translate the breakwater and vessel into the ribbed remains of a colossal timber horse and the direction of departure; preserve the pale sky and steel-blue sea.
11. Spiderweb, single center, and blurred green background → PENELOPE’S LOOM. Translate the web into a system repeatedly woven and unwoven, retaining only one restrained golden knot of waiting at the center; do not depict a spider.
12. Clock tower, glass reflection, sloped corridor, and enclosing branches → THE WAITING HOUSE. Translate the tower into a small marker of time, the glass layers into repeated transparent planes of waiting, and the diagonal light into a beeswax passage.
13. Autumn woods, foreground leaves, one nearby person, and four distant walkers → LOTUS SHORE. Preserve the one-to-four count relationship, translate the fallen leaves into drifting membrane layers, and the people into nodes of lingering and departure; use natural autumn light.
14. Squirrel descending a tree trunk, a single vigilant gaze, and a broad field of bark → ATHENA KEEPS WATCH. Make the trunk the dominant vertical body and retain only one observing eye-point and the downward direction; do not depict a complete squirrel or owl.
15. Pale historic building, central tower, repeated arches, and dense vehicles → HALL OF SUITORS. Make the tower an occupied axis of power, translate the vehicles into irregular dense modules, and introduce localized beeswax light inside the arches; preserve the source’s pale gray-pink identity.
16. Flame, charcoal, orange-red heated edges, and black background → DESCENT TO THE UNDERWORLD. Translate the flames into ascending membrane bands and a descending dark river, with an incomplete doorway marking the threshold of Hades; fire must reveal charcoal, smoke, and rock, while dead black remains controlled.

11. ENGLISH LABELS AND RELIABLE TYPESETTING

First generate a completely text-free abstract lower panel. Then use a reliable typesetting or compositing tool to place both panels on the unified near-black canvas and add the following to the bottom black title band:

1. One concise archive number, for example NO. 015.
2. One date, for example 23 AUG 2026.
3. One correctly spelled English episode title of two to four words.

Do not add actors, directors, film titles, locations, camera settings, extra copy, watermarks, or pseudo-text. Set the number and date in smaller gray-gold or warm gray type, and set the English episode title in clear bone white. Align all three to the left using the same margin. Verify every character of the title before submission.

12. FAILURE CHECKS AND CONDITIONS FOR REGENERATION

If any of the following occurs, regenerate only the lower panel. Do not alter the upper photograph:

- the lower panel merely repaints the source photograph or applies a painterly filter;
- its principal colors are unrelated to the source;
- it contains large areas of dead black and its candlelight reveals no material;
- smoke, grime, grain, scratches, or unmotivated debris make the image dirty;
- it contains only one isolated central icon while the rest of the field is unorganized;
- its mythic symbol looks like a sticker, souvenir, or video-game monster;
- a turtle shell, eye, fur, or other animal detail has been enlarged into a kitsch ornament;
- unrelated motifs such as the Trojan Horse, Cyclops, vortex, and temple are stacked together;
- the upper panel has been cropped, redrawn, color-graded, or covered;
- the title is misspelled or contains gibberish, pseudo-text, or conflicting titles.

13. FINAL CHECK

Before submission, confirm that:

- the upper panel is exactly identical to the uploaded original photograph;
- every principal form in the lower panel is traceable to photographic evidence;
- the chosen episode is supported by at least two or three pieces of evidence;
- at least three recognizable groups of source colors remain in the lower panel;
- the iconic motif is fused with the source structure;
- the seam feels natural without covering the upper panel;
- the single finished work uses one unified near-black outer canvas, restrained margins, and one bottom title band;
- the archive number, date, and English title are clear, correct, and unique.

The final work should resemble a page from a lost maritime archive recast as a contemporary art book: the upper panel preserves reality as evidence, while the lower panel lets the same spatial relationships resurface as a visual memory of the Odyssey.

Output the final complete diptych directly. Afterward, add only one or two sentences stating which episode was selected, which evidence in the photograph supports that choice, and why the work uses natural light, firelight, or overcast/liminal light.

[COMMON CORRECTION PROMPTS]

If the lower panel is too dark:
Regenerate the lower panel. Preserve detail in stone, wood, smoke, water, and metal within the shadows, and let candlelight reveal real volume. Reduce near-black coverage to approximately 30% or less and fully dead-black coverage to approximately 12% or less. Do not alter the upper photograph.

If the colors do not match the source:
Re-extract the source photograph’s dominant colors, shadows, midtones, highlights, and accent. Use the source color system across 70%–85% of the lower panel, and remove unmotivated blue-orange, black-and-gold, or fluorescent color.

If the image is too literal:
Remove complete silhouettes of people, animals, architecture, and landscape. Preserve only counts, spacing, directions, apertures, routes, color bands, nodes, pressure, and light relationships.

If the mythic element looks pasted on:
Remove decorative symbols. Reintegrate the selected motif into structures already present in the source by expressing it through scale, count, enclosure, pressure from both sides, vortex, woven lines, or bow-like tension.

If the composition feels too empty:
Establish one dominant large structure, two to four groups of medium rhythm, and a small number of tiny nodes so the image has an entry, a tension zone, and an exit direction.

If a turtle shell or animal element looks kitsch:
Remove complete shell patterns, eyes, fur, or claw ornaments. Preserve only the animal’s real count, spacing, posture, and direction, translating them into restrained seed-like nodes or small coordinates.

If the upper photograph has been altered:
Undo every generative treatment applied to the upper panel and restore the original photograph’s pixels. Generate only the lower panel, then assemble the result with a deterministic compositing tool.

If the title is wrong:
Remove all generated text from the image. Keep the artwork text-free first, then use a reliable typesetting tool to add one archive number, one date, and one correctly spelled English episode title.
